Saturday Special · Two days to the bell

The champions are back. This time the opponent is Kaggle.

NVIDIA’s CPMP and Ivan Sorokin won ARC Prize 2025. Now they’re back with two teams, the best Kaggler who ever lived, and a platform that keeps eating their submissions. Meet the competitors.

Saturday, October 31, 2026Boards read 1:58 pm EasternDeadline: Monday, 11:59 pm UTC. No extension.

Two days to the bell, and the final weekend of ARC Prize 2026 has turned into a stress test of Kaggle itself. In ARC-3, competitors report submissions dying with system errors, or finishing in half an hour with a flat zero. In ARC-2, scores take more than a day to come back, so last-day entries may never count.

The hosts say the deadline stands. We told you so Friday night. And nobody was louder about it than the man who knows what winning here looks like.

That’s the story. These are the players.

NVIDIA: the champions return

Last year Jean-François Puget, CPMP, and Ivan Sorokin won ARC Prize as NVARC, first of 1,455 teams. This year NVIDIA fields two entries in two contests. In ARC-2, CPMP and Darragh Hanley are nvbanana, third on the board at 83.89. In ARC-3, seven players make up NVARC3, eighth at 49.89 and inside the gold line.

Saturday morning CPMP quoted our no-extension bulletin and gave the weekend a name: circus. His diagnosis is his own. Kaggle opened a new Gemma contest on the same graphics-card pool, he says, and ARC-3’s storage is drowning in giant-model variants. Kaggle’s Walter Reade answers that resource use across Kaggle is up two to three times since the ARC contests launched, and Kaggle staff say they can’t look at individual submissions right now.

Tufa’s detective on the case

Friday night we put Jeroen Cottaar on the GPU throne. Saturday he’s dusting for fingerprints. Tufa Labs is first in ARC-2 at 88.06 and second in ARC-3 at 70.02, with Yi-Chia Chen’s Fields Model the mirror image: second in one, first in the other. The top of both boards belongs to those two.

Cottaar’s finding: Kaggle is quietly eating about forty minutes of every submission. An empty notebook with nothing attached takes around fifty minutes to start, he reports, yet real runs still stop at the nine-hour mark, so the missing time comes out of the game. He ran it on two container images and from two continents.

Enter CPMP, who says a delay before a notebook runs is nothing unusual. Cottaar answers with the empty-notebook test. No villains, just two of the sharpest minds in the field, each holding a stopwatch.

V | A | M: the climbers

Points, not places. Chan Kha Vu’s team took its score from 50 points to 76 in five days. On Saturday’s board that 76.25 is ninth place in ARC-2, inside the gold line. The target Chan named is 80 and beyond.

The roster lists arazzd, chankhavu and nguyennguyen599, and Chan’s post tags @nguyen_manh599 and @razdaibi. On the circus, Chan has made the cleanest argument in the field: with scoring taking more than a day, a last-day submission can’t finish in time. The ask isn’t more submissions, it’s more time to pick a final one.

And an old score: Chan’s team took second at the 2026 NeuroGolf Championship. First place went to Giba, who plays this year for NVARC3.
