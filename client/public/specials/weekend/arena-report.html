When the arena flickers

Two contests, two different headaches, one deadline that isn’t moving. What’s going wrong, who’s saying what, and what it means for Monday night.

Saturday, October 31Reported with sources linkedEvery cause belongs to whoever named it

The short version

Everybody’s still in the arena

ARC Prize 2026 runs two contests on Kaggle at once, and both have been straining in the final stretch. In ARC-3, players say submissions have crashed with system errors or come back with a flat 0.00 after half an hour. In ARC-2, players say scores are taking more than a day to arrive. Kaggle’s staff say they are working on the problems and can’t look at individual submissions right now. The hosts say the deadline stands: Monday, November 2, 11:59 pm UTC, which is 6:59 pm Eastern.

Nobody in this story is a villain. The Kaggleverse is a huge, crowded arena, and on its busiest weekend of the year the lights are flickering. The legends are still in it.

ARC-2

The long wait

ARC-2 entries run on Kaggle’s L4x4 machines: four NVIDIA L4 chips apiece, with up to twelve hours per run. Kaggle’s own contest page says the machines are in short supply. They use up a player’s weekly quota at twice the usual rate, and they are reserved for notebooks attached to ARC-2.

When the line gets long, a twelve-hour run waits its turn. Competitors say the queue has passed twelve hours and scores are taking more than a day to come back, which means an entry sent late on Monday may not be scored before the bell. Chan Kha Vu of V | A | M put it plainly in the forum: a last-day submission is invalid under those conditions, and the ask is more time to pick a final one, not more submissions. A player called masspeaks proposed a fixed selection window after the deadline, and CPMP backed a fixed window. Kaggle’s María Cruz told players the deadline and the submission process won’t change in the final days.

CPMP flagged the L4 queue a month ago. Kaggle’s Dustin answered that demand had increased considerably and added capacity. CPMP’s own theory this week is that a Gemma contest is drawing on the same L4 pool. Kaggle hasn’t confirmed that, and the ARC-2 page says the L4x4 machines are reserved for ARC-2, so the puzzle is still open.

ARC-3

The zeros

ARC-3 entries run on RTX 6000 machines with nine hours per run. Over the past two weeks players have reported three kinds of trouble: saves and submissions that fail with a “Kaggle error” when a big model can’t be loaded; runs that finish as “complete” after about half an hour with a flat 0.00; and long, unpredictable waits before a run even starts.

Kaggle’s staff said a bad cache in one region was cleared about a week ago, that the team is looking into broader platform problems, and that for now they can’t investigate individual submissions because of capacity constraints. Kaggle’s Walter Reade, who works on AI at Kaggle, said load across the whole site is two to three times higher since the ARC contests launched, with AI agents making it easier than ever to enter a contest.

Tufa’s Jeroen Cottaar timed the startup wait. With an empty notebook it takes about fifty minutes before anything runs, he says, and real runs still stop at nine hours, so the wait comes out of the game. CPMP’s reply: a delay before a notebook runs isn’t unusual. Cottaar answered with the experiment. Two Grandmasters, one stopwatch, no villains.

Players have adapted. xz, whose one-person team is third on the board, starts the games at the ten-minute mark whether or not the model server is up and resets every seven minutes. Van-Phuc Huynh trades solving time for longer waiting to avoid a zero.

The hosts

What the hosts say

ARC Prize says the deadline remains Monday, November 2, 11:59 pm UTC, and that it is working with Kaggle on the slowdowns. That was our Friday-night report. Kaggle’s contest pages add a caution: the organizers reserve the right to update the timeline, and any change would be posted under Overview, Timeline.

Monday

What it means for the final hours

Each team can pick up to two final submissions; if a team doesn’t pick, Kaggle picks for it. The private leaderboard decides the final standings. Players are asking whether a run sent before the bell but scored after it will count. Kaggle’s staff have said only that the deadline and the submission process stay as they are. The Final Hours has the rest.
