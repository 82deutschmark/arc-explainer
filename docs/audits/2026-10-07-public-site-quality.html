ARC Explainer: visitor quality audit

Cleanup implemented in version 9.119.1. This document preserves the pre-fix evidence. See the cleanup and validation record.

Checked 7 October 2026, Eastern time · published source d78ef060 · Read-only audit

The site has working, useful resources. Its most urgent credibility defects are inaccurate editorial claims, inconsistent metric scopes, and pages that overstate their completeness. Eleven concrete findings follow. These are reproduced observations, with editorial judgments identified as such.

No production changes were made during this audit. The Human ARC deployment completed in the preceding task remains live.

What works

  • The ARC-3 play journey loaded a real game and responded to input. Gallery thumbnails and filters presented their groups correctly.
  • The dated Human/AI comparison distinguishes completion, score, best-of-settings selection, and top-10 human winners. The Kaggle page labels public standings and private settlement.
  • Model Comparison populated its selectors and results. Two Worm Arena replays loaded and played.
  • The new Human ARC assessment and library were checked during deployment. Its guest dashboard correctly says no performance data for an unsolved guest.

Verified findings

First

1. Homepage gives ARC Prize unfair credit and oversimplifies the harness result

Open affected page

Evidence: The homepage and chart caption say ARC Prize offered no interpretation. The official Astra article has an Analysis section and Two Harnesses, Two Questions. Standard preserves selected visible notes; Provider Adapter additionally preserves opaque reasoning state and uses compaction.

Why it matters: This is the most avoidable credibility problem when sharing the site with the ARC Prize team.

Repair: Keep the comparison and chart, credit the published analysis, describe both harnesses accurately, and label the causal interpretation as an interpretation.

2. Scoring mixes an available-results score with full-dataset counts

Open affected page

Evidence: Default GPT-5.2 High displays 61.8%, 41/120 fully solved puzzles and 63/167 pairs. The API and source show the score averages 76 puzzles. The explanation says all 120 count and missing puzzles score zero. These are incompatible descriptions. Model Comparison uses the same calculation.

Why it matters: Visitors cannot reproduce the headline score from the scope the page claims.

Repair: First label the actual available-results subset and its coverage, and remove the incorrect full-dataset/stricter-scoring assertion. Before publishing a corrected full-dataset score, reconcile each task against authoritative test.length; do not merely change the aggregate denominator.

Source: server/repositories/MetricsRepository.ts:847-886; client/src/components/analytics/AttemptUnionCard.tsx:233

3. Confidence is displayed above 100%

Open affected page

Evidence: Trustworthiness leaders show Grover confidence 7410.0%, Qwen 7070.0%, and several entries at 5000.0%. The renderer multiplies the already-percent confidence field by 100.

Why it matters: An immediately visible numerical error makes the broader analytics look unreliable.

Repair: Format confidence on its documented 0–100 scale, separately from the 0–1 trustworthiness score. Add a focused regression check for these two units.

Source: client/src/components/overview/leaderboards/TrustworthinessLeaderboard.tsx:177

4. AS66 is called fully documented while its rule descriptions are missing

Open affected page

Evidence: The page shows Fully documented, then No rules written up for this game yet. Subsequent levels turn empty rule arrays into a statement that nothing new occurs. It also gives the generic official-play instruction despite the game being withdrawn.

Why it matters: A reader cannot distinguish documented sameness from missing knowledge.

Repair: Mark documentation partial, say no additional rules are documented when that is all the data establishes, and use retired-game-specific guidance. Do not invent the missing mechanics. The official human leaderboard link was reachable with no entries; this is not reported as a broken link.

Source: shared/arc3Games/as66.ts:76; client/src/components/arc3/gamePage/LevelSection.tsx:119

5. Scoring costs describe only the first attempt beside combined results

Open affected page

Evidence: The score combines two attempts; Total Cost comes from modelNames[0]. Cost per Correct is the average cost of successful runs, not all spending divided by successes. The default view showed $89.4147 total and $0.3891 per correct.

Why it matters: The presentation can suggest an efficiency comparison that its cost scope does not support.

Repair: Label existing figures as first-attempt metrics and define the successful-run mean, or calculate both attempts consistently. Do not silently relabel one calculation as another.

Additional editorial questions, not proven failures

  • The public-game index suggests learning those games prepares visitors for the private set, whose mechanics are deliberately different. Limit the promise to learning the interface and format. Its Chollet quotation needs a source; this audit did not establish that the wording is false.
  • Human ARC promises cognitive-performance scoring and improvement in fluid intelligence without an on-page validation source. Describe what is actually measured—performance on these puzzles—and treat broader learning claims as claims requiring evidence.

Concrete repair plan

  1. Correct the homepage attribution and harness explanation, the confidence scale, the AS66 documentation state, preview history, and the time-statistic wording.
  2. Make scoring/cost labels describe the data currently displayed. Remove the untrue full-dataset claim and unqualified champion language. Do not present a denominator-only edit as a verified scoring repair.
  3. Repair the old Human ARC link, unimplemented sign-in link, developer 404 wording, and literal escape. Label the old Resource Hub honestly; a redesigned directory can follow separately.
  4. Then audit score computation against authoritative puzzle test counts, with missing-task and multi-test examples, before changing numerical score semantics.
  5. Build, verify affected live journeys in a preview, and publish a single reviewed cleanup release.

Repository instructions require approval before implementation. This document makes that cleanup scope concrete; it is not a request to rewrite game mechanics, redesign the whole site, or change database records.
